# Arounus growth plan

## Positioning

Help South African students practise for their next assessment, reach a real tutor, and find useful campus services in one place. Lead public campaigns with a specific subject and campus; present the wider platform after the first useful result.

## First audience

Start with one campus and two or three subjects where Arounus has reliable tutors and enough quality questions. Grade 12 Chemistry and Physics or existing university modules are candidates. Select the first group using actual tutor capacity, content coverage, and learner demand. Expand only after the first group's learners complete quizzes and get timely tutor replies.

## Acquisition

1. Publish subject pages with the curriculum, a worked example, a sample quiz, tutor availability, and a clear next step. Use descriptive titles such as “Grade 12 Chemistry revision and tutors in South Africa.”
2. Give tutors shareable links to a specific quiz or lesson. Invite departments, student societies, and study groups to distribute useful free revision material. Do not claim an institutional partnership without one.
3. Produce short revision videos from questions students repeatedly miss. Each video should lead to its matching quiz, not a generic homepage.
4. Invite real campus sellers and tutors before promoting the market. Keep examples labeled and out of live-offer counts.
5. Ask learners for a review after they receive a marked result or completed tutor session. Never purchase or fabricate reviews.

## Return visits that help students

- Recommend an unfinished quiz or a short review of a weak skill.
- Show the reason for the recommendation and the estimated effort.
- Give immediate explanations and link a missed question to a lesson or tutor.
- Offer optional reminders chosen by the learner. Make them easy to pause. Avoid loss-framed streaks and excessive notifications.
- Show weekly progress in skills mastered, not minutes spent in the app.

## Trust and marketplace operations

- Verify tutor identity and relevant qualifications before displaying a verified badge.
- Show price, availability, response expectations, sponsorship, and what happens if a tutor does not attend.
- Separate examples from active listings in counts, ranking, and advertising.
- Review listings for stale availability and provide a simple way to report a problem.
- Explain pricing and renewal terms before any payment step.

## Measurement and experiments

Primary measure: learners who improve on a later attempt at the same skill. Supporting measures: first quiz completion, seven-day return rate, tutor response time, completed tutor sessions, listing contact rate, and reported safety issues. Segment by campus, subject, device, and data usage.

Run one change at a time. For example, compare the current dashboard with a specific unfinished-quiz recommendation. Keep the change only if completion and later learning improve without more complaints or unwanted notifications. Avoid interpreting a small sample as proof; report sample size and uncertainty.

## Release order

1. Correct subject assignments and tutor access; verify marketplace examples are unmistakable.
2. Fill gaps between quizzes and lessons; improve feedback on missed questions.
3. Launch one focused campus and subject campaign, then measure learning and tutor response.
4. Add optional reminders and progress summaries once the core learning loop works reliably.
